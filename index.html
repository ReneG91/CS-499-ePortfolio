Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout my Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have developed a stronger understanding of software development, programming, algorithms, databases, and cybersecurity. When I first started pursuing my degree, my goal was to transition from a career in management into the technology field. That goal has remained consistent, but my coursework has helped me better understand the different areas of computer science and where I want to focus my career. I have become particularly interested in software engineering, artificial intelligence, and game development. I enjoy creating applications, identifying problems, and finding ways to make software more efficient and useful.

My professional background has also played an important role in preparing me for a career in computer science. I have spent years working in leadership positions where communication, organization, decision-making, and problem-solving were part of my daily responsibilities. Managing teams taught me how to identify priorities, work through unexpected challenges, and communicate expectations to people with different levels of experience. These skills are just as important in software development, where projects often require collaboration between developers, stakeholders, and other departments. My education has allowed me to combine that leadership experience with the technical knowledge needed to develop software solutions.

Collaboration and communication are two areas where I believe my previous experience and education complement each other. In CS 250, Software Development Lifecycle, I learned how Agile and Scrum methodologies support teamwork, project planning, and continuous improvement. Working through different Scrum responsibilities helped me understand how developers, testers, and project stakeholders contribute to the same goals. My coursework also required me to communicate technical information through written reports, diagrams, presentations, and project documentation. These experiences strengthened my ability to explain technical decisions in a way that different audiences can understand.

My understanding of algorithms and data structures has improved considerably throughout the program. In CS 300, I worked with vectors, linked lists, hash tables, and binary search trees while evaluating how different structures affect performance and efficiency. These concepts helped me understand that there is often more than one way to solve a programming problem, and choosing the right approach can have a significant impact on an application's performance. I have learned to consider efficiency, maintainability, and scalability instead of focusing only on whether the code produces the expected result.

Software engineering and database development have been especially valuable areas of my education. I have worked with Java, Python, C++, JavaScript, and other development tools while creating applications for different platforms. In CS 465, I developed components of a full-stack travel application using technologies such as Node.js, Express, Angular, and MongoDB. In CS 340, I worked with Python and database operations to create an interactive dashboard. These experiences taught me how the front end, back end, and database work together to support application functionality. They also reinforced the importance of designing software that is organized, maintainable, and useful to its intended audience.

Security is another area that has influenced how I approach software development. In CS 305, Software Security, I examined vulnerabilities and implemented security improvements involving encryption, certificates, and secure communication. I learned that security should be considered throughout the development process rather than treated as something to address after an application is completed. Input validation, protecting sensitive information, reviewing dependencies, and reducing potential vulnerabilities are all important parts of developing reliable software. I plan to continue applying these principles as I work on future projects.

My CS 499 ePortfolio brings these different skills together through three enhanced software artifacts. The Android Sensor Application demonstrates software design and engineering through improved motion detection and acceleration processing. The CS 320 Task Application demonstrates algorithms and data structures through task management, searching, and sorting. The Android Inventory Application demonstrates database development through SQLite integration, inventory searching, low-stock filtering, and data validation. Each project represents an opportunity to revisit previous work, identify weaknesses, and apply the knowledge I have gained throughout the program.

Completing this degree has given me more confidence in my ability to transition into a technology-focused career. I understand that there is still a great deal to learn, especially as new tools and technologies continue to emerge. My goal is to begin working in software development while continuing to expand my knowledge of artificial intelligence, game development, and other areas that interest me. I believe my combination of leadership experience, technical education, and willingness to continue learning will allow me to contribute to a development team and grow as a computer science professional.
